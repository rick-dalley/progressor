1. The Statutory Criteria
The officer must explicitly document why they believed the individual met the legal threshold for detention. This includes:

Presence of a Mental Disorder: Evidence (observed or reported) that the person appeared to be suffering from a mental disorder.

Immediate Need: Why the officer felt the person required "immediate care or control."

Public/Personal Safety: Whether the detention was necessary in the interests of that person's own safety or for the protection of others.

2. Behavioral Observations (The "Why Now?")
This is the most critical section for triage nurses. It contains raw, objective descriptions of the events leading to the handoff:

Specific Actions: e.g., "Subject was standing on the outside of the bridge railing," or "Subject was shouting at non-existent figures and lunging at passersby."

Verbal Statements: Direct quotes of any threats of self-harm, suicidal ideation, or delusional statements made by the individual.

Physical State: Documentation of any injuries, signs of drug or alcohol intoxication, or evidence of self-neglect.

3. Risk Assessment and History
The report will detail any immediate risks the hospital staff needs to manage:

Markers of Violence: Did the person struggle, use a weapon, or make specific threats toward the officers?

Intelligence Checks: Information pulled from police databases regarding previous mental health crises, history of violence, or known triggers.

The "Place of Safety": Confirmation that the individual was found in a place to which the public has access (a requirement for Section 28).

4. Search and Property Record
For the safety of the ward, the report will state:

Whether the person was searched.

A list of confiscated items (e.g., pocket knives, lighters, medication, or illicit substances) that are being handed over to hospital security or the nursing staff.

5. Logistics and Timings
Because Section 28 has a strict legal time limit (usually 24 hours, extendable by another 12 in specific circumstances), the report must clearly state:

The exact time of initial detention in the community.

The time of arrival at the hospital (the "Place of Safety").

This "starts the clock" for the medical team to complete their assessment.
